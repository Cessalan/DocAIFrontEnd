The NCLEX has evolved. In April 2023, the National Council of State Boards of Nursing (NCSBN) launched the Next Generation NCLEX (NGN), introducing new question types designed to better assess clinical judgment. Here's everything you need to know.

## What Changed with Next Generation NCLEX?

### The Clinical Judgment Measurement Model (CJMM)

The NGN is built around a new framework that measures how nurses make clinical decisions. The NCSBN Clinical Judgment Measurement Model includes six cognitive skills:

1. **Recognize Cues** - Identify relevant information
2. **Analyze Cues** - Determine significance of information
3. **Prioritize Hypotheses** - Rank likely explanations
4. **Generate Solutions** - Identify expected outcomes and interventions
5. **Take Action** - Implement appropriate interventions
6. **Evaluate Outcomes** - Assess effectiveness of interventions

### Why the Change?

Research showed that new graduate nurses were struggling with clinical judgment at the bedside. The traditional NCLEX wasn't adequately measuring these critical thinking skills. The NGN addresses this gap.

## New Question Types on the NGN

The NGN introduces several new item types. Let's break down each one:

### 1. Extended Multiple Response (Select All That Apply - Enhanced)

Similar to traditional SATA, but may include:
- More answer options (up to 10)
- Partial credit scoring
- Instructions to select a specific number of responses

**Example:**
"Select the 4 nursing actions appropriate for this patient."
- [ ] Option A
- [ ] Option B
- [ ] Option C
- [ ] Option D
- [ ] Option E
- [ ] Option F

**Strategy:** Read carefully for specific instructions. The question may tell you exactly how many to select.

### 2. Extended Drag-and-Drop

Drag items to complete a table, chart, or sequence.

**Types include:**
- Cloze (fill-in-the-blank with drag options)
- Rationale building
- Treatment sequencing

**Example:**
"Drag the appropriate assessment findings to the correct body system."

| Cardiovascular | Respiratory | Neurological |
|---------------|-------------|--------------|
| [Drop zone] | [Drop zone] | [Drop zone] |

**Strategy:** Read all options first. Use process of elimination.

### 3. Drop-Down Cloze

Select answers from drop-down menus within a passage or table.

**Example:**
"The nurse should first assess the patient's [dropdown: vital signs / pain level / lab values] before administering [dropdown: morphine / aspirin / oxygen]."

**Strategy:** Read the entire passage before selecting. Consider how each selection affects the meaning.

### 4. Highlight Text

Highlight specific words, phrases, or sentences in a passage.

**Example:**
"Highlight the findings in the nurse's notes that require immediate follow-up."

*Nurse's Notes: Patient is a 45-year-old admitted for CHF exacerbation. Current VS: BP 90/60, HR 110, RR 24, SpO2 88% on room air. Patient reports shortness of breath that has worsened since admission. Bilateral crackles heard on auscultation. 2+ pitting edema noted in lower extremities.*

**Strategy:** Look for abnormal values, changes from baseline, and safety concerns.

### 5. Matrix/Grid

Answer multiple questions in a grid format.

**Example:**
"For each finding, indicate if it is consistent with left-sided heart failure, right-sided heart failure, or both."

| Finding | Left-sided | Right-sided | Both |
|---------|-----------|-------------|------|
| Dyspnea | ○ | ○ | ○ |
| Peripheral edema | ○ | ○ | ○ |
| Fatigue | ○ | ○ | ○ |
| JVD | ○ | ○ | ○ |

**Strategy:** Consider each row independently. Use your knowledge of pathophysiology.

### 6. Bowtie Items

A specialized format that tests the full clinical judgment process.

**Structure:**
- Center: Patient situation
- Left side: Potential conditions/causes
- Right side: Actions to take

**Example:**
Center: "Patient presents with sudden onset chest pain, diaphoresis, and left arm numbness."

Left (Select 2 most likely conditions):
- [ ] Pulmonary embolism
- [ ] Myocardial infarction
- [ ] Pneumonia
- [ ] Anxiety attack

Right (Select 2 priority actions):
- [ ] Obtain 12-lead ECG
- [ ] Administer antianxiety medication
- [ ] Position for comfort
- [ ] Administer aspirin

**Strategy:** Start with the center situation. Think about what conditions fit the presentation, then what actions match those conditions.

### 7. Case Studies (Unfolding Case Studies)

A series of questions about a single patient that "unfolds" over time. You'll see the same patient in multiple scenarios.

**Structure:**
- Stand-alone questions: 6-question case studies
- NGN items: May include various item types within one case

**Example progression:**
1. Initial assessment findings
2. Question about recognizing cues
3. Additional information provided
4. Question about prioritizing hypotheses
5. Patient status changes
6. Question about evaluating outcomes

**Strategy:** Pay close attention to timing and changes. New information may change the best answer.

## Scoring Changes

### Partial Credit

Some NGN items offer partial credit:
- Getting some correct answers = partial points
- Selecting wrong answers may deduct points
- Final score still pass/fail

### Variable-Length Testing

The NCLEX still uses Computerized Adaptive Testing (CAT):
- Minimum: 85 questions (up from 75)
- Maximum: 150 questions (up from 145)
- Time limit: 5 hours

## How to Prepare for the NGN

### 1. Master the Clinical Judgment Process

Practice thinking through patient situations systematically:

**For every case, ask yourself:**
- What information is relevant? (Recognize Cues)
- What does this information mean? (Analyze Cues)
- What could be causing this? (Prioritize Hypotheses)
- What outcomes do we want? (Generate Solutions)
- What should I do first? (Take Action)
- How will I know if it worked? (Evaluate Outcomes)

### 2. Practice Case-Based Thinking

Don't just memorize facts. Practice applying knowledge to patient scenarios.

**Exercise:** For any disease process you study, work through:
- What would I assess?
- What findings would I expect?
- What findings would concern me?
- What would I do about it?
- How would I know my interventions worked?

### 3. Get Comfortable with New Question Types

Practice with NGN-style questions before exam day:
- Use practice tests that include NGN items
- Don't panic when you see unfamiliar formats
- Read instructions carefully—they tell you what to do

### 4. Focus on Common Clinical Scenarios

High-yield topics for NGN:
- Acute changes in patient condition
- Delegation and prioritization
- Medication administration decisions
- Lab value interpretation
- Post-procedure assessments
- Patient deterioration recognition

### 5. Practice Time Management

With more complex questions, time management is crucial:
- Don't spend too long on any single question
- Case studies: Budget 10-15 minutes max
- Individual items: 1-2 minutes each

## Sample NGN Practice Questions

### Highlight Question

Read the nurse's notes. Highlight the findings that require immediate follow-up.

*1400: Patient is a 67-year-old male, post-op day 1 following right hip replacement. Patient reports pain at surgical site rated 6/10. VS: BP 138/88, HR 76, RR 18, SpO2 96% on RA, Temp 99.1°F. Patient ambulated 50 feet with physical therapy this morning without incident. Right leg appears edematous with calf tenderness. Patient denies shortness of breath. Surgical dressing clean, dry, and intact.*

**Answer:** "Right leg appears edematous with calf tenderness" should be highlighted. This could indicate DVT, which is a serious post-operative complication requiring immediate assessment.

### Drop-Down Cloze

Complete the following statement by selecting from the dropdown options.

The nurse caring for a patient with suspected myocardial infarction should first [obtain vital signs / administer aspirin / call the physician] and then immediately [start oxygen therapy / obtain a 12-lead ECG / draw cardiac enzymes].

**Answer:** First: obtain vital signs (assessment first), Then: obtain a 12-lead ECG (confirm diagnosis)

### Matrix Question

For each medication, indicate whether it is appropriate or not appropriate for a patient with heart failure and renal impairment (GFR 28).

| Medication | Appropriate | Not Appropriate |
|------------|-------------|-----------------|
| Furosemide | ○ | ○ |
| Metformin | ○ | ○ |
| Lisinopril | ○ | ○ |
| Spironolactone | ○ | ○ |

**Answers:**
- Furosemide: Appropriate (loop diuretics are used in HF)
- Metformin: Not Appropriate (contraindicated in renal impairment)
- Lisinopril: Use with caution (may worsen renal function, but beneficial in HF)
- Spironolactone: Use with caution (risk of hyperkalemia with renal impairment)
