What dosage calculation exams test

  • Tablets and capsules: how many to give
  • Oral liquids and injections: how many mL
  • Weight-based doses in mg/kg, including lb to kg conversion
  • IV infusion rates in mL/hr
  • Manual drip rates in gtt/min using the drop factor
  • Heparin and other unit-based infusions

Question 1 · Multiple choice
The order is amoxicillin 500 mg PO. The pharmacy supplies 250 mg tablets. How many tablets should the nurse give?
  1. 1 tablet
  2. 2 tablets
  3. 2.5 tablets
  4. 0.5 tablet
Show answer and rationale

Answer: B

A. One tablet gives only 250 mg.

B. Correct. 500 mg ÷ 250 mg × 1 tablet = 2 tablets.

C. Would give 625 mg.

D. Would give 125 mg; the ratio is inverted.

Question 2 · Multiple choice
The order is digoxin 0.125 mg PO. Available: digoxin 0.25 mg scored tablets. How many tablets?
  1. 0.5 tablet
  2. 1 tablet
  3. 2 tablets
  4. 0.25 tablet
Show answer and rationale

Answer: A

A. Correct. 0.125 mg ÷ 0.25 mg = 0.5 tablet.

B. Would give 0.25 mg, double the order.

C. Would give 0.5 mg, four times the order.

D. Would give 0.0625 mg.

Question 3 · Multiple choice
The order is amoxicillin 250 mg PO. Available: amoxicillin suspension 125 mg/5 mL. How many mL should the nurse give?
  1. 2.5 mL
  2. 5 mL
  3. 10 mL
  4. 20 mL
Show answer and rationale

Answer: C

A. Would give 62.5 mg.

B. Would give 125 mg, half the order.

C. Correct. 250 mg ÷ 125 mg × 5 mL = 10 mL.

D. Would give 500 mg.

Question 4 · Multiple choice
The order is morphine 4 mg IV. Available: morphine 10 mg/mL. How many mL should the nurse draw up?
  1. 0.4 mL
  2. 2.5 mL
  3. 4 mL
  4. 0.04 mL
Show answer and rationale

Answer: A

A. Correct. 4 mg ÷ 10 mg × 1 mL = 0.4 mL.

B. This is have over desired, inverted.

C. Would give 40 mg, a tenfold overdose.

D. Decimal error; gives 0.4 mg.

Question 5 · Multiple choice
A child weighs 44 lb. The order is acetaminophen 15 mg/kg PO. What dose should the child receive?
  1. 150 mg
  2. 300 mg
  3. 660 mg
  4. 30 mg
Show answer and rationale

Answer: B

A. Uses 10 kg; 44 lb is 20 kg.

B. Correct. 44 lb ÷ 2.2 = 20 kg; 20 kg × 15 mg/kg = 300 mg.

C. Multiplied pounds by 15 without converting to kg.

D. Arithmetic error.

Question 6 · Multiple choice
The order is 1,000 mL normal saline IV over 8 hours on an infusion pump. At what rate should the nurse set the pump?
  1. 80 mL/hr
  2. 100 mL/hr
  3. 125 mL/hr
  4. 150 mL/hr
Show answer and rationale

Answer: C

A. Would deliver 640 mL in 8 hours.

B. Would deliver 800 mL in 8 hours.

C. Correct. 1,000 mL ÷ 8 hr = 125 mL/hr.

D. Would deliver 1,200 mL in 8 hours.

Question 7 · Multiple choice
The order is 1,000 mL lactated Ringer's over 8 hours by gravity. The tubing drop factor is 15 gtt/mL. What is the drip rate, rounded to the nearest whole drop?
  1. 21 gtt/min
  2. 31 gtt/min
  3. 42 gtt/min
  4. 125 gtt/min
Show answer and rationale

Answer: B

A. Arithmetic error.

B. Correct. 1,000 mL × 15 gtt/mL ÷ 480 min = 31.25, rounded to 31 gtt/min.

C. Would need a drop factor of 20.

D. This is the mL/hr rate, not drops per minute.

Question 8 · Multiple choice
Heparin 25,000 units in 500 mL D5W is ordered to run at 1,000 units/hr. At what rate should the nurse set the pump?
  1. 10 mL/hr
  2. 20 mL/hr
  3. 40 mL/hr
  4. 50 mL/hr
Show answer and rationale

Answer: B

A. Would deliver 500 units/hr.

B. Correct. Concentration is 25,000 ÷ 500 = 50 units/mL; 1,000 units/hr ÷ 50 units/mL = 20 mL/hr.

C. Would deliver 2,000 units/hr.

D. This is the concentration in units/mL, not a rate.

How to get every dosage calculation right

  1. Use one method every time: (desired ÷ have) × quantity. Mixing methods causes errors under pressure.
  2. Convert units before you calculate: lb to kg (÷ 2.2), g to mg (× 1,000), mg to mcg (× 1,000).
  3. Ask whether the answer makes sense. More than 3 tablets, or more than 3 mL for an IM injection, usually means a mistake.
  4. Leading zero always (0.4 mL), trailing zero never (4 mg, not 4.0 mg).
  5. Know your program's rounding rules; drip rates round to whole drops.

Frequently asked questions

What is the formula for dosage calculation?

Desired dose divided by the dose you have on hand, multiplied by the quantity it comes in: (D ÷ H) × Q. Dimensional analysis gives the same answer with the units written out.

How do you calculate a drip rate?

Total volume in mL multiplied by the drop factor in gtt/mL, divided by the time in minutes. Round to a whole drop.

What score do you need on a nursing dosage calculation exam?

Many programs require 90 to 100%, often with limited retakes, because a calculation error in practice is a medication error.
