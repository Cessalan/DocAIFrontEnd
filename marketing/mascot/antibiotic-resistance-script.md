# Pip explains: antibiotic resistance (script draft)

Status: DRAFT, needs nurse review before anything is generated for publishing.
Format: 9:16, 35-42s, mascot Pip (marketing/mascot/option-c-vinyl-toy.png).

Changes from the original script, and why (from the 2026-11-04 viral read):
- The opening myth goes on screen as large text in the first 3 seconds. The winning
  health-character videos all open with a big on-screen claim.
- The patient advice is framed as what nurses teach, so it speaks to nursing students
  and not only patients.
- The ending is a nursing-student question answered in the comments. NCLEX-style
  quiz posts grow through comments; the original has no reason to comment.
- "the bacteria causing your infection" became "an infection" because the viewer is
  now the nurse, not the patient.

## Version A (recommended): original voice-over, student ending

| # | Time | Voice-over | On-screen text (hook / caption emphasis) |
|---|------|-----------|------------------------------------------|
| 1 | 0-4s | Your body doesn't become resistant to antibiotics. The bacteria do. | **MYTH: your body becomes resistant** (struck through) |
| 2 | 4-6s | I'm Pip, and here's how. | |
| 3 | 6-13s | An antibiotic kills most of the bacteria causing an infection. But a few may carry a mutation that helps them survive. | |
| 4 | 13-19s | Those survivors multiply, and can pass that resistance to other bacteria. | |

| 6 | 29-34s | And remember, they don't work on colds or flu. Those are viruses. | |
| 7 | 34-40s | Nursing students: your flu patient wants to take last year's leftover amoxicillin. What do you tell them? | **What do you tell them? Comment below** |
| End | 40-42s | (music) | NurseQuizAI logo + "Practice questions like this free at nursequizai.com" |

About 112 spoken words, roughly 40s at a calm explainer pace.

Answer for the pinned comment (needs nurse sign-off):
> Antibiotics treat bacteria, not viruses like the flu, so they won't help and can
> cause side effects and resistance. Leftover antibiotics should never be saved or
> taken without a prescriber's direction. Encourage them to contact their provider
> about flu treatment and symptom relief.

## For the nurse reviewer: lines to check

1. "An antibiotic kills most of the bacteria": some antibiotics stop growth rather
   than kill (bacteriostatic). Acceptable simplification for a 40s video?
2. "a few may carry a mutation": resistance also spreads by gene transfer (covered by
   line 4). Is the order clear enough?
3. "follow the directions": deliberately not "finish the whole course", since current
   guidance is to take them as the prescriber directs. Agree?
4. The pinned-comment answer above.
5. The call to action in the end card (offer and URL).
