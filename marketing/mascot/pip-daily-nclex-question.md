# Pip's Daily NCLEX Question (series format draft)

Status: DRAFT. Every question and answer needs nurse review before posting.

## The format (modelled on @robdoesnursing, Daily NCLEX Question 037: 1.9M views, 103x his median)

What his post does:
- The hook IS the question: on screen from frame 1, no intro, no voice.
- One static shot of someone in scrubs "thinking" (looking down, writing).
- Music only, a popular track.
- 7-10s, built to loop, so viewers re-read and watch it 2-3 times.
- No answer in the video. The caption says "Comment your answer", so the comments
  fill up with answers and arguments.
- Numbered ("Daily NCLEX Question 037"), so it reads as a series to follow.

The Pip version:
- Pip (marketing/mascot/option-c-vinyl-toy.png) at a small desk with a clipboard,
  tapping a pen, glancing up and back down. One 5-6s loop generated once and reused
  for every episode. Only the text changes daily.
- Top band: "🚨 Pip's Daily NCLEX Question 001".
- Middle band (inside the safe zone): the question and its options, large.
- No voice-over. A soft bed rendered in, then a trending sound added in the
  TikTok/IG app at upload (licensed sounds can't be burned into the file).
- Caption: "Comment your answer 👇 Answer pinned tomorrow".
- Answer + rationale pinned as a comment the next day (keeps people coming back).

Cost: one animated Pip loop, about 80 Runway credits, reused for the whole series.
After that each episode costs nothing to make (text + ffmpeg).

## 001: Dosage calculation (closest to Rob's 1.9M post)

> Ordered: amoxicillin 250 mg PO
> Available: amoxicillin oral suspension 125 mg/5 mL
> How many mL do you give?

Answer: **10 mL** (250 ÷ 125 × 5).

## 002: Antibiotic resistance, patient teaching

> A client with a viral upper respiratory infection asks for an antibiotic "just in
> case." Which response by the nurse is best?
> A. "I'll ask the provider to prescribe one so you're covered."
> B. "Antibiotics don't treat viruses, and taking them when not needed can lead to resistance."
> C. "You can take the leftover antibiotics from your last infection."
> D. "Start an antibiotic if your fever lasts more than a day."

Answer: **B**. Antibiotics act on bacteria, not viruses. Unneeded use adds side
effects and selects for resistant bacteria. A and D promote inappropriate use; C is
self-medicating with leftovers.

## 003: Select all that apply, new antibiotic teaching

> Which instructions should the nurse include when teaching a client about a new
> antibiotic prescription? Select all that apply.
> A. Take it exactly as prescribed.
> B. Do not share it with anyone else.
> C. Save any leftover pills for a future infection.
> D. Stop taking it once you feel better.
> E. Report a rash or difficulty breathing right away.

Answer: **A, B, E**. C and D are both unsafe. E covers allergic reaction and
anaphylaxis.
